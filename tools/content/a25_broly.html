Broly, the Legendary Super Saiyan from Dragon Ball, is the biggest body in the series: massive chest, enormous traps, legs like tree trunks. He's pure mass. If you're a hardgainer who's sick of being the skinny one, Broly is the right inspiration, because his look is built by the same two things that build real size: eating a lot and training heavy with plenty of volume. This 12-week mass program is designed for skinny guys who want to gain serious weight with dumbbells at home.

How Real Mass Is Built

Size has a simple recipe, and most skinny guys only follow half of it:

  • A consistent calorie surplus. You can't build a big body out of nothing. Broly mode means eating 400–600 calories above maintenance every day.
  • Heavy compound lifts. Squats, deadlifts, presses and rows recruit the most muscle at once.
  • Enough volume. 12–20 hard sets per muscle per week, spread over several sessions.
  • Time. Twelve weeks gets you a clear transformation; a year gets you a different body.

The 12-Week Legendary Mass Program

Four days a week, upper/lower split, with heavier days and higher-rep pump days. You need adjustable dumbbells that go reasonably heavy (50 lb+ per hand helps), a bench and a pull-up bar.

Two minutes of light cardio, 10 bodyweight squats, 10 push-ups, 10 band pull-aparts, then two ramp-up sets of your first lift (about 40% and 70% of your working weight).

Eating Like the Legendary Super Saiyan

This is the part that decides everything. Get your bulking number from the calorie calculator and add 100–200 more if you're a true hardgainer. Aim to gain 0.5–1 lb (0.25–0.5 kg) per week. Protein at 0.7–1 g per pound of bodyweight (1.6–2.2 g per kg), lots of carbs (rice, oats, potatoes, bread) and easy fats (olive oil, peanut butter, whole milk). If you struggle to eat enough, a mass gainer or a homemade shake from the high-calorie smoothie recipes adds 600–1,000 calories in a glass. The full method is in the hardgainer bulking guide.

Common Mistakes

  • Eating big on training days only. Muscle grows on rest days too. Hit your number every day.
  • Program hopping. Twelve weeks of the same plan beats four programs in twelve weeks.
  • Too little sleep. Seven to nine hours. Broly's rage doesn't replace recovery.
  • Fear of gaining a little fat. A slow bulk adds some fat along with the muscle. That's normal and easy to trim later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much weight can I gain in 12 weeks?

Most beginners gain 6–12 lb (3–5 kg) in 12 weeks of consistent training and a surplus, with a large share of it muscle. Experienced lifters gain slower.

Do I need a mass gainer?

No, but it helps if you can't eat enough food. Homemade shakes with milk, oats, peanut butter and banana work just as well and cost less.

Can I build a big body with only dumbbells?

Yes, especially as a beginner or intermediate. You'll need dumbbells that go heavy enough to keep progressing; an adjustable set that reaches at least 50 lb per hand covers years of training.

Is Broly's body possible?

No. His size is far beyond human limits. A big, strong, muscular build is absolutely achievable with years of consistent training and eating.
