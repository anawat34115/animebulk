Yujiro Hanma, "the Ogre" from Baki, is drawn with the most extreme muscle in anime: a back so developed it forms a demon's face. No human will ever look like that. But the idea behind it is useful, because a big, powerful back is the single most impressive thing you can build in a home gym, and it's what makes a physique look strong from every angle. This 12-week plan is a back-specialization program: everything grows, the back grows most.

Why the Back Is the Key to Looking Powerful

Your back is the biggest group of muscles in your upper body. A wide, thick back makes your waist look smaller, your shoulders look broader and your posture look confident. It also protects your spine and carries every heavy thing you'll ever lift. Most home trainees neglect it because they can't see it in the mirror. Yujiro's whole design is a reminder that the back is where real strength shows.

The Muscles You're Building

  • Lats: width. Built with pull-ups, chin-ups and pullovers.
  • Traps and rhomboids: thickness in the upper back. Built with rows, shrugs and carries.
  • Rear delts: the 3D look from behind. Built with reverse flyes and face pulls.
  • Spinal erectors: the "Christmas tree" in the lower back. Built with Romanian deadlifts and back extensions.

The 12-Week Ogre Back Program

Four days a week: two back-focused days, one push-and-legs day, and one full-body day. You'll train your back three times a week with different stimuli, which is where the growth comes from.

Two minutes of light cardio, 15 band pull-aparts, 10 scapular pull-ups, 10 cat-cows, 10 bodyweight good mornings and one light set of your first exercise.

Pull with your back, not your arms. On every row and pull-up, start by pulling your shoulder blades down and back, then bend your elbows. Lower slowly and let your lats stretch at the bottom. Half-reps with a swinging torso build forearms and ego, not a back.

The Demon Back Is Fiction. A Great Back Isn't

Yujiro's "Demon Back" is drawn far beyond human anatomy. What you can build is a back with visible lats, thick traps and a deep spinal groove, which is what most people mean when they say someone "looks strong." Expect visible changes in 8–12 weeks and a genuinely impressive back after 1–2 years of training it hard three times a week.

Eating to Build a Big Back

Back size takes calories. Eat 300–500 calories above the maintenance number from the calorie calculator, with protein at 0.7–1 g per pound of bodyweight (1.6–2.2 g per kg). If gaining weight is hard for you, the hardgainer bulking guide explains how to eat more without forcing it, and creatine monohydrate (3–5 g a day) helps you squeeze out the extra reps that drive growth.

Common Mistakes

  • Only doing pull-ups. Pull-ups build width. Rows, shrugs and Romanian deadlifts build thickness. You need both.
  • Using momentum on rows. If your torso moves more than your elbows, the weight is too heavy.
  • Ignoring rear delts. They're small, but they're what make a back look 3D from the side and behind.
  • Never checking your back. Take a photo of your back every four weeks in the same light. It's the only way to see progress you can't see in the mirror.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Yujiro Hanma's body possible?

No. His muscles, especially the Demon Back, are drawn far beyond human limits. A wide, thick, well-developed back is absolutely achievable with consistent training, and it's the closest real-world version.

Can I build a big back at home?

Yes. Pull-ups, dumbbell rows, Romanian deadlifts, shrugs and carries cover every back muscle. A pull-up bar and adjustable dumbbells are enough for years of progress; a bench makes chest-supported rows and pullovers possible.

How often should I train my back?

Two to three times a week with 12–20 hard sets in total works for most people. This plan hits it three times with different exercises so each session is a fresh stimulus.

Why does my lower back get sore?

Some soreness after Romanian deadlifts and back extensions is normal in the first weeks. Sharp pain isn't: reduce the weight, keep your back flat and hinge at the hips. If pain persists, see a professional.
