Izuku Midoriya starts My Hero Academia as a skinny kid with no powers and ten months to build a body that can survive One For All. All Might's beach-cleaning plan turns him from a twig into a solid, athletic teenager. That's the most relatable training arc in anime, and it's exactly what a beginner needs: a structured plan with a deadline. This 10-week program is the real-life version, built for complete beginners who are starting from zero.

What the Beach-Cleaning Arc Gets Right

  • A clear goal and a deadline. Deku has ten months to pass the U.A. entrance exam. Goals with dates get done.
  • A coach-built schedule. All Might writes the "Aim to Be American Dream Plan," covering training, sleep and food. Every part matters.
  • Carrying heavy things. Dragging refrigerators and tires off a beach is loaded carries and sled work: some of the best full-body strength training there is.
  • Eating properly. All Might plans his meals. You'll use the calorie calculator for the same reason.

What It Gets Wrong

Deku overtrains so badly that All Might has to rewrite the plan, and the series makes it clear he breaks his body repeatedly. Real beginners need rest days, gradual progression and a plan they can recover from. Ten weeks with three training days and one conditioning day does more than ten months of exhaustion.

The 10-Week "Plus Ultra" Plan

Three full-body strength days and one carry-and-conditioning day a week. Every session takes 40–50 minutes. You need a pair of adjustable dumbbells and a doorway pull-up bar; a backpack you can load with books works for the carry day.

Two minutes of brisk walking or jumping jacks, 10 arm circles each way, 10 bodyweight squats, 10 glute bridges and 5 slow push-ups from your knees.

Deku's arms break because he uses 100% of a power his body isn't ready for. Your version: never jump more than one weight increment at a time, and stop a set when your form breaks, not when your muscles give out completely.

The Beginner's Results Timeline

Beginners get the fastest results of anyone who lifts. In the first 3–4 weeks most of your strength gains come from your nervous system learning the movements, so the weights go up quickly. By week 6–8, muscle you can see starts to show in your shoulders, arms and upper back, especially if you're eating enough. By week 10 you'll be noticeably stronger and fill out your shirts differently. That's your U.A. entrance exam passed.

Eat Like All Might Planned It

If you're skinny like early Deku, you need a calorie surplus to build muscle. Get your bulking number from the calorie calculator, eat 0.7–1 g of protein per pound of bodyweight (1.6–2.2 g per kg), and spread it over three meals and two snacks. Common Mistakes

  • Training every day because Deku did. Three strength days plus one carry day is the right amount for a beginner.
  • Skipping legs. All Might made Deku drag refrigerators. Your legs are your biggest muscles and the fastest way to put on weight.
  • Not sleeping. Growth happens during sleep. Seven to nine hours is part of the plan.
  • Quitting in week 3. Results feel slow at first. Take a photo in week 1 and week 10, same light, same pose.

Frequently Asked Questions

How did Deku get so muscular?

In the story, ten months of All Might's training plan: clearing a beach full of heavy trash, strict meals and lots of sleep. The real-world equivalent is full-body strength training three times a week, loaded carries and a calorie surplus.

Is this plan good for complete beginners?

Yes, it's designed for them. Every exercise has an easier version, the weights start light, and the progression is slow on purpose.

Can teenagers do this workout?

Strength training is safe and beneficial for teenagers when form comes first and weights stay moderate. Anyone under 18 should get a parent's okay and ideally a coach or PE teacher to check their form.

What if I can't do a pull-up?

Almost no beginner can. Use a resistance band around your knee or do negatives: jump to the top and lower yourself over 5 seconds. Most people get their first full pull-up within 6–10 weeks.
