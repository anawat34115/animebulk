Mikasa Ackerman from Attack on Titan has one of the most admired physiques in anime, especially the abs that became a meme. She's lean, strong and athletic: a soldier's body built for gear that pulls her through the air at high speed. This 8-week plan builds that kind of physique for anyone, with a focus on core strength, pulling power and full-body athleticism. It works for women and men; the training is the same, only the weights differ.

The Mikasa Physique, Translated

  • A visible, strong core. Built with real core training (anti-extension, anti-rotation, hanging work) and low body fat, not 500 crunches.
  • A strong back and arms. ODM gear means hanging, pulling and controlling your body. Pull-ups and rows are the heart of this plan.
  • Athletic legs and glutes. Landing, jumping and sprinting need strong hips. Squats, lunges, hip thrusts and jumps.
  • Conditioning. Scouts don't get tired halfway through a mission.

The 8-Week Survey Corps Plan

Four days a week: two full-body strength days, one core-and-pull day and one athletic conditioning day. You'll need adjustable dumbbells, a pull-up bar and a resistance band.

Two minutes of jump rope or jumping jacks, 10 leg swings each direction, 10 glute bridges, 10 scapular pull-ups, 10 bodyweight squats and a 20-second plank.

Add weight on strength days when every set hits the top of the rep range. On core day, progress the exercise rather than the reps: knee raises become leg raises, plank walkouts become ab wheel rollouts, hollow holds get longer. Week 5 is lighter (two sets each); weeks 6–8, beat your week-4 numbers.
Core training is about control, not burn. Keep your lower back flat on hollow holds and rollouts; if it arches, shorten the range. A strong, braced core is what protects your spine when you lift heavy.

The Truth About Visible Abs

Everyone has abs; they only show when body fat is low enough. For most men that's around 10–14% body fat, and for most women around 18–22%. Training builds the muscle, so your abs look thicker and more defined when they appear. Nutrition reveals them. Crunches alone will never give you Mikasa's midsection; a strong core plus a modest calorie deficit will. Just as important: very low body fat isn't healthy or sustainable for everyone, especially women, and a strong body at a healthy weight is the real goal.

Eating for a Lean, Strong Build

Find your maintenance calories with the calorie calculator. If you want more definition, eat 200–400 calories below maintenance; if you're already lean and want more muscle, eat 200–300 above. Protein stays high at 0.7–1 g per pound of bodyweight (1.6–2.2 g per kg) either way, which helps you keep muscle while you lean out and stay full on fewer calories.

Common Mistakes

  • Training abs every day. Core muscles need recovery too. One focused day plus the core work built into the other days is plenty.
  • Avoiding heavy weights. Lifting heavy builds the strong, athletic look. It won't make you "bulky" by accident.
  • Crash dieting for abs. A big deficit costs you muscle and energy. Small deficit, high protein, patience.
  • Skipping pull-ups because they're hard. Bands and negatives get everyone there. Your first strict pull-up is a milestone worth chasing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this workout good for women?

Yes. It's built for anyone who wants a lean, strong, athletic body. Start with lighter dumbbells and band-assisted pull-ups, and progress at your own pace.

How do I get abs like Mikasa?

Train your core with harder variations over time (hanging leg raises, ab wheel rollouts, hollow holds), lift heavy compound exercises, and lower your body fat with a small calorie deficit and high protein.

How many times a week should I train core?

One dedicated core session plus core work in two other sessions is enough for most people. Quality and progression matter more than frequency.

Can I do this without a pull-up bar?

You can swap pull-ups for band pulldowns and inverted rows under a sturdy table, but a doorway pull-up bar is cheap and makes the plan much better.
