Ryomen Sukuna, the King of Curses in Jujutsu Kaisen, is drawn with a lean, ripped and dangerous physique: wide shoulders, a sculpted back and arms, and a waist with zero softness. In the manga's Heian-era form he has four arms, which no program can give you. What you can build is the "dangerous and lean" look: high muscle definition, a strong back and a pulling-dominant upper body. This 8-week plan is a lean-muscle program built on pull work, shoulders and a small calorie deficit.

The Sukuna Look: Lean Muscle, Not Bulk

Sukuna (in Itadori's body) isn't a bodybuilder. He's a fighter with very low body fat and dense, visible muscle. That look comes from three things working together:

  • Strength training that keeps muscle while you lean out. Heavy compound lifts tell your body to hold onto muscle in a calorie deficit.
  • Back and shoulder emphasis. A wide, detailed back and capped shoulders make a lean body look powerful instead of thin.
  • Low body fat. Definition is mostly a nutrition result. You'll eat slightly below maintenance with high protein.

The 8-Week King of Curses Plan

Four days a week on an upper/lower split with a pull emphasis, plus optional walks. You need adjustable dumbbells, a pull-up bar and ideally a bench.

Two minutes of brisk walking or jump rope, 15 band pull-aparts, 10 scapular pull-ups, 10 bodyweight squats, then a light set of your first lift.

On the other days, walk 7,000–10,000 steps. Walking burns fat without eating into recovery.

Your goal is to keep or slowly increase your strength while body fat drops. If your lifts hold steady for 8 weeks while your waist shrinks, the plan is working perfectly. Add weight when every set hits the top of the rep range; if strength drops two weeks in a row, eat 150 more calories.
Getting lean is not getting thin. Don't slash calories hard or add hours of cardio. A small deficit with heavy lifting keeps the muscle that makes the "dangerous" look; a crash diet strips it away.

Eating for Definition

Get your maintenance number from the calorie calculator and eat 300–500 calories below it. Push protein to the top of the range, about 1 g per pound of bodyweight (2.2 g per kg), because high protein protects muscle in a deficit and keeps you full. Aim to lose 0.5–1% of your bodyweight per week. If you're skinny and have no fat to lose, skip the deficit: eat at maintenance or slightly above and run the plan for strength and detail.

Common Mistakes

  • Chasing a six-pack with crunches. Hanging leg raises and heavy lifts build the abs; the deficit reveals them.
  • Dropping weights to "tone". Keep lifting heavy. Light weights in a deficit cost muscle.
  • Skipping rear delts and back detail. They're what make a lean body look carved instead of skinny.
  • Going too fast. More than 1% of bodyweight a week is usually muscle loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get a body like Sukuna?

Build muscle with heavy compound lifts and a pull-focused upper body, then lower your body fat with a small calorie deficit and high protein. The ripped look appears when you're lean enough for definition to show.

How lean is Sukuna?

He's drawn at very low body fat with visible abs and vascular arms. For most men that's around 10–12% body fat, which takes months of consistent training and eating to reach and isn't necessary for a strong, healthy body.

Can I build muscle while losing fat?

Beginners and people returning to training often can ("body recomposition"). Experienced lifters usually maintain muscle while losing fat. Either way, heavy training and high protein are the keys.

Do I need cardio?

Daily walking is enough for most people. Lifting four days a week plus a calorie deficit does the heavy lifting; hard cardio is optional.
